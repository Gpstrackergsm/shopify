A quick clinical observation that comes up frequently in trauma work:

When a client suddenly shuts down, dissociates, or changes the subject during a session, the initial instinct is often to gently push through or attempt more grounding.

However, through the lens of Internal Family Systems (IFS) and structural dissociation, that shutdown is almost always an active, loyal Protector part doing its job to shield a vulnerable exile.

Instead of trying to bypass the resistance, acknowledging the protector directly often unlocks the entire session:

"Let's step back for a moment. Can we thank this part of you that just showed up to protect you? What is it worried might happen if we keep exploring this?"
